# IPVV — How to freestyle this text: LESSONS so far (running)

*2026-08-11. A running, lived-in record of what works when converting the IPVV into readable prose.
Updated as each passage is done. The method is: raw Sanskrit → L0 → L1 → ARGUMENT MAP → L2 (freestyle
READ). Lesson = an operational rule that survives contact with the text.*

## L1. Reconstruct the argument, not the sentence
The single biggest fix. IPVV is often opaque because it compresses a *whole dialectical exchange*
into one sentence (nanu + reply + counter-reply). Translating word-by-word produces "with the
dealing-mere-param-essence-ness…" nonsense. **Always write the ARGUMENT MAP first**: speaker, the
question at issue, premises, inference, conclusion, unresolved terms. The readable prose then writes
itself and is actually correct.
- Evidence: S7 (anvaya-vyatireka) — "wtf" under L1, coherent under argument-map. Same for S9, K6.

## L2. Identify the *question* the passage answers
Every passage is doing a job. Name it before writing. Examples:
- S7: "what makes a cognition true?" → efficacy; defended against the Buddhist on causality/momentariness.
- S9: "is the conventional just fiction?" → no; it's coordinate with the real, anchored in usage.
- K6: "are number & measure real features?" → no; they're relational appearances of counting/comparison.
Naming the question fixes the frame and stops the prose from drifting.

## L3. The Buddhist opponent is always there (and often wins the round)
This is a Buddhist-bashing / Buddhist-co-opting text. When a passage reads as "flaw / objection /
'not even so'", assume: Abhinava states the Buddhist view fairly, *then* turns it. Do not read the
objection as Abhinava's own view. Speaker-tag ruthlessly.

## L4. Don't invent realia — but DO say what the argument needs
When you hit a technical/realia term (Kāṇāda measures, agrarian lexemes, ritual instruments), the
*argument* is almost always recoverable even when the *referent* isn't. Preserve the argument, flag
the gap, never fake the magnitude/meaning. E.g.:
- dāntava/līvarda: the point (bull-words vs. cow-word = the conventional is anchored in specifics) is
  clear; the lexemes are left open.
- parimāṇa/pramāṇa/unmāna: the point (measure = comparison against a standard) is clear; the exact
  magnitudes are not resolved.
Better to say "the lightest of the mustard-seed, the pod, the small box" and flag it than to invent
a unit value.

## L5. The bhedābheda / saṃvṛti thesis is the load-bearing frame
Almost every section of this chunk (and much of the IPVV) returns to one move: **the one enters the
many; the conventional is coordinate with the real, not a sub-species of the illusory; difference and
non-difference are not contradictory because the cognition is unbroken.** Naming this frame early
(the "thus" of the whole) lets you read forward- and backward-references correctly (kārikā 1's
stability/efficacy recurs in kārikā 7's "stability-use two").

## L6. Dyczkowski-mode is: continuous philosophy + bracketed interpolation
Don't glue glosses. Render the kārikā's meaning as flowing prose; put only what a reader *needs* to
follow the claim in square brackets (a supplied referent, an implied opponent, the standard against
which measure is taken). Brackets are load-bearing, not decoration. Show where a quoted source comes
in (MVV, vi. bhai., Kāṇāda, nyā., vā. pa.), then the note.

## L7. Fidelity + honesty are separate gates
- **Fidelity:** every L1 proposition present in L2; nothing added (or, if added, it's SUPPLIED and
  recoverable); no polarity/speaker change.
- **Honesty:** if you cannot explain the paragraph aloud in plain English, do NOT produce L2 — say
  "argument unresolved." Do not smooth an obscure passage into false clarity. (None of S7/S9/K6 hit
  this wall; all three were resolvable once the argument map was done.)

## L8. The `-ness` tell is a diagnostic
When L1/L2 is stuffed with "-ness" (dealing-mere-param-essence-ness), that's a sign you are still in
controlled mode and have not yet named the underlying proposition. Dissolve it: "X-ness" = "the fact
that X is…" or "X's being…". The readable version names the proposition; it doesn't transliterate the
suffix.

## L9. Read it aloud test
After writing L2, read it straight through as a book (no Sanskrit in view). If a sentence makes you
pause to reconstruct the syntax, it isn't done. If the *argument* makes you pause (a real crux), that's
different — log it, don't hide it.

## L10. Volume 1 (M00020) is untranslated
The 35 chunks cover Vol 2 (V2-A..S) and Vol 3 (V3-A..P). **Volume 1 (M00020, 8313 lines) needs a full
T1 translation before any L2.** That's a separate, large task; the same argument-map method should be
applied once T1 exists.

## L11. Kill the template verdict (2026-08-11)
The "Verdict: understood and readable. X argues cleanly that…" closer became a fill-in-the-blank
formula repeated verbatim across 27 L2 files. It reads as boilerplate and adds no value. **Rule: do
not end an L2 read with a canned verdict.** Close instead with a line that is specific to *that*
passage — the actual twist, the key image, or the seam into the next section — or with no formal
verdict at all. The "Fidelity note" already records what was preserved/added/unresolved; the READ prose
should close the way a good paragraph of philosophy closes, not the way a report does.
