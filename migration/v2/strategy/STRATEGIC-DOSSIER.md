## 1. THE THESIS (what Pāṭala IS)

**One line:** Pāṭala is the **authority, provenance, relationship, expert-validation and workflow
layer** that turns digitised tantric/Sanskrit material into usable scholarly knowledge for humans and
machines. *[source: `docs/NORTHSTAR.md`]*

**The strategic opening:** Pāṭala does **NOT** compete with the incumbents — it sits *between* them:

> "We have thousands of scans, catalogue records and transcriptions. Tell us what these objects
> actually are, how they relate to known works and witnesses, what scholarship exists, which claims
> have been human-validated, what rights apply, and how researchers and AI systems can use them."
> *[NORTHSTAR]*

**What Pāṭala is NOT** (the anti-positioning):
- not another archive · not a Sanskrit library · not a manuscript-digitisation project · not a
  translation publisher · not "search Sanskrit". *[`docs/positioningpartners.md`, `NORTHSTAR`]*

**The cleanest frame:** Pāṭala as **"OpenAlex for Sanskrit"** — not "search Sanskrit" but
**resolve the Sanskrit intellectual record.** *[`docs/global/globalpartnerships.md`]*

## 2. THE ECOSYSTEM POSITION (placement)

> **Pāṭala is the trusted reconciliation, provenance, and scholarly verification layer for Sanskrit —
> not the free bulk-download layer.** *[`docs/global/globalaccess.md`]*

The durable moat is not "nobody knows our facts"; it is **"everybody depends on our resolution and
provenance."** *[globalaccess]*

**The public identity:**
> "Pāṭala provides persistent identities, provenance, and scholarly resolution for Sanskrit texts,
> manuscripts, editions, interpretations, and arguments. We connect existing collections rather than
> replace them." *[globalaccess]*

That tells IFP, Muktabodha, GRETIL and PANDiT that Pāṭala is **complementary, not extractive.**

**Access model: "Open-Reference, Controlled-Corpus"** — publicly discoverable, privately deep. You can
hold a strong ecosystem role without exposing the whole corpus through a public API. *[globalaccess]*

## 3. THE PARTNER ECOSYSTEM (who we partner with, why)

Pāṭala preserves every external custodian as the authoritative source; it connects them. *[globalpartnerships]*

### The four partner classes

1. **Manuscript custodians** (own the physical witnesses): IFP Pondicherry + EFEO (~8,500 palm-leaf
   codices, disproportionately Śaiva Āgamas) · Muktabodha (3,000+ texts, 570+ e-texts, tailored to the
   initial vertical) · NGMCP/NGMPP (180,000+ microfilmed manuscripts) · BORI Pune · National Mission for
   Manuscripts / IGNCA · Bodleian (~8,700, clean Data API + IIIF) · Cambridge UL (1,600+). *[globalpartnerships]*

2. **Existing digital Sanskrit projects** (become sources/adapters, never replaced): GRETIL/SARIT/
   Ambuda (machine-readable) · BDRC/BUDA (the mature Buddhist analogue) · Sanskrit Heritage (linguistic
   analysis). *[globalpartnerships, NORTHSTAR]*

3. **Translation/publishing incumbents** (not to compete with): 84000 · SuttaCentral/Bilara. *[NORTHSTAR]*

4. **Scholars & institutions** (the human layer): BHU (Faculty of Arts: Sanskrit; Philosophy & Religion)
   · global Indology/philosophy scholars (Sanderson, Pollock, A. Gupta) · IIAS Shimla · IIT Bombay DH ·
   IGNCA · digital-humanities centres. *[`docs/vision/vision-10-market-entry-and-partnerships.md`]*

**For the Śaiva launch specifically:** IFP + EFEO + Muktabodha + NGMCP are disproportionately valuable.
*[globalpartnerships]*

## 4. THE ECONOMICS (how Pāṭala is sustained)

**The core thesis:** Pāṭala builds the institutional coherence the tantric field lacks, sits on
genuinely scarce assets, and converts scholar participation into data capital and revenue. The
economics pays the scholars whose corrections are the moat. *[`docs/vision/economics/README.md`]*

### The scarce assets (5 years out) *[`docs/endgame4.md` §0, economics/README]*
```
unique source data      manuscript imaging + transcription + alignment datasets
rights                  legitimate digitisation + AI-data access
provenance              claim → translation → Sanskrit → manuscript witness
expert judgment         the thing no one can clone
trusted relationships   the scholar + institutional network
human corrections       continuously generated supervisory data
```

**The economic thesis is NOT "we raise money to translate obscure texts."** It's: build the
company/institution around the scarce assets. *[endgame4]*

### The 84000 lesson *[endgame4]*
The Buddhist analogue wasn't a VC bet — it was credible leader + credible scholars + mission, seeded by
~108 founding sponsors ($5M). **Pāṭala's funding path mirrors this: mission + scholars + institutional
seed, not feature-SaaS.**

### Scholar incentives (the fatal-framing warning) *[`docs/vision/vision-08-scholar-economics.md`]*
> Get scholars in early, BEFORE Pāṭala becomes "the AI translation project scholars are supposed to
> correct for free." That framing would be fatal.

The scholar must feel: **"Pāṭala is giving me leverage, credit, money, data, and a platform for my
scholarship"** — not "someone generated 100,000 lines with AI and wants me to clean it up."

**The model: paid adjudication + durable academic credit + genuine intellectual ownership.**
- **Pay for the scarce work, not generic reviewing.** Tightly scoped **scholarly bounties** ("review
  this argument"), not "could you review our IPVV?"
- Credit via **ORCID / CRediT / DOI**.
- **Not "free AI cleanup for scholars."**

## 6. GO-TO-MARKET (market entry) *[vision-10]*

**Pilots (low-cost, credibility-first):**
- **(a) Gold Corpus Lab** — small team hand-annotates 5 key debate texts → mini-corpus + report
- **(b) Annotation sprints** — weekend "annotation hackathons" to tag premises/attacks
- **(c) Summer school** — 1-2 week "Computational Sanskrit Philology" workshop (BHU or IIAS Shimla)
- **(d) Micro-fellowships** — small stipends ($2K) to 2-3 scholars

**12-month roadmap:** gold-corpus + expert workshops (Q3-Q4 2026) → first public demo site
(Q1-Q2 2027) → conference presentation (Q3 2027).

**Channels:** conferences (DH, Orientalist, Philology) · journals (JIP, Digital Humanities Quarterly) ·
networks (ADHO, EADH, Sahapedia) · social (#Dharmavicara, LinkedIn).

**Metrics:** scholar engagement · data output (propositions/arguments) · academic impact (citations) ·
community growth · institutional support (grants, partnerships).

## 8. THE FIRST-PRODUCT DOCTRINE *[`docs/global/patala-full-audit-bundle/FIRST_PRODUCT_DECISION.md`]*

The market/placement doctrine says: **start with the narrowest honest product that creates structured
correction data and builds credibility.**

- **First public scholar product: Translation Audit** — immediate job-to-be-done; starts honestly with
  deterministic checks; exposes source/provenance work; creates structured correction data; feeds the
  benchmark; easier adoption than a Workbench; narrower failure surface than generic peer review.
- **First strategic asset: PĀṬALA-IPVV Benchmark** — measures own claims; creates research credibility;
  attracts model builders; converts expert judgment into defensible data; private locked set = durable
  evaluation value.
- **First internal engine: Autonomous Factory** — continuously creates difficult cases; gives
  Audit/Benchmark real material; lets Pāṭala optimise for human review burden.
