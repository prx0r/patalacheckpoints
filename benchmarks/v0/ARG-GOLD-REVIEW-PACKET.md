## ARG-GOLD-002 — The Non-constructed I (objection → reply, V2-L)
**Passage:** `pt:passage:ipvv:chunkV2-L-sastho-vimarsa-smrti-apohana.md` · **Structure:** OBJECTION_REPLY

### SOURCE (the material a reviewer judges against)
**Commentary (C1):**
The objection is straightforward: if reflexive awareness is joined to linguistic form, why is it not
simply a conceptual construction (*vikalpa*)? Abhinavagupta answers by asking what construction
actually does. The forms considered involve combining, differentiating, or determining contents. The
awareness expressed as "I," however, is not treated as one more relation constructed between
independently given elements. The point is therefore not merely that the "I" is linguistically
unusual; it is that linguistic articulation does not show that the underlying self-awareness is
itself produced by conceptual determination. This allows Abhinavagupta to preserve a distinction
between reflexive self-awareness and the conceptual operations that articulate it.

The stronger Śaiva conclusion — that this self-awareness belongs ultimately to a single universal
subject — is not established by this passage alone and depends on arguments elsewhere.

## ARG-GOLD-004 — The Essence of Light: prakāśa vs vimarśa (V2-H)

**Research question:** Is the essence of light bare showing (prakāśa) or reflexive awareness (vimarśa)?

### SOURCE (the material a reviewer judges against)
**Commentary (C1):**
The claim is that the essence of light is the reflexive awareness, not the bare showing of objects.
The crystal supplies the contrast: a light that merely showed the world without knowing that it showed
it would be no different from crystal — it would reflect, but be inert. What makes the light conscious
is the self-awareness in the very act of manifesting. The passage then identifies this self-knowing
with the supreme speech (parā-vāk) and with the Lord's freedom and lordship.

The identification with the Word is the extension: the reflexivity is not a bare self-presence but
the supreme speech from which the lower levels of language descend. This is why the word-joined "I"
need not be a construction — language is the form the self-grasp takes, not something that produces
it. The passage establishes the reflexivity claim; the full account of the powers and the Lordship is
developed in the following vimarśas.

## ARG-GOLD-005 — The Difference is Real: local vs systematic scope (V3-I)
**Passage:** `pt:passage:ipvv:chunkV3-I-kriya-caturtho-close-k20-21.md` · **Structure:** INTERPRETIVE_SCOPE
**Research question:** What does 'the difference is real' mean — does the passage positively affirm difference as the self's manifestation, or negatively establish it by refuting the 'un-explainable ignorance' thesis?

### SOURCE (the material a reviewer judges against)
**Commentary (C1):**
The sharpest objection to the unity: if consciousness is one and the difference of things is mere
ignorance, how is action possible? For action needs an agent and an object, distinct; but if all is
one, there is no distinct object. Abhinavagupta first answers with the will-to-do — the single
re-recollection marked by the will that joins the agent and the object.

Then he challenges the premise that the difference is ignorance. If the difference were an
"un-explainable" ignorance, of whom would it be? The one-form reality cannot have an ignorance that
presupposes a duality to be absent from; the separate souls cannot bear it, since they are supposed
to be produced by it; and "un-explainable" is a refusal to answer, not an answer. The difference is
real: cognition rests in difference as it does not rest in non-difference.

The passage establishes that the difference cannot be an un-explainable ignorance. The full positive
account of the difference (as the self's own manifestation) is developed elsewhere.
